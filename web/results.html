Dark Vessel Finder

Turning Sentinel-1 radar and AIS into dark-ship alerts that an analyst can trust, tested on 23 real frames over Danish waters.

Arnav Deshpande · IIT Indore github.com/Arnavdsp/dark-vessel-finder

The problem

A dark-ship alert is a radar detection with no AIS report to explain it. Matched naively, honest ships look dark: a ship moving towards or away from the satellite is drawn hundreds of metres from its true position, and a bright ship leaves a faint ghost kilometres away along-track. On real data, wind turbines, buoys and coastline edges add thousands more alerts, so most alerts are not dark ships and an analyst has to dig through them.

This project corrects the radar geometry before matching, removes known structures, and ranks what is left by how much it looks like a ship. Geometry comes from each product's annotation: slant range, incidence and azimuth time per pixel, and the pulse repetition frequency of each of the three sub-swaths.

1. Moving ships are where the physics says they are

For each moving ship with an AIS report less than a minute old and exactly one radar detection nearby, I measured how far along-track the detection sits from the AIS position and compared it with the predicted shift, −(R/V)·vrange·sin θ.

2. Ghosts appear at the predicted distance and nowhere else

A negative control: count bright/faint pairs of detections (at least 12 dB apart, within 40 m in range) at k times the predicted ambiguity offset λ·R·PRF/(2V). If the ghosts are real, the count should peak at k = 1 only.

3. What each step removes

Open water more than 2 km from the coast, inside Danish AIS reception (within 40 km of Denmark and closer to Denmark than to any other country). There is no ground truth for "dark", so the scores are ones AIS can check: how many AIS ships end up paired, and how many alerts sit within 1.5 km of an AIS ship the matcher left unpaired (that ship, drawn in the wrong place).

4. Ranking what is left

The remaining alerts are mostly coastline edges, land texture and wakes, with real point targets among them. There is no label for a dark ship, but every AIS-paired detection is a ship. A classifier trained to separate AIS-paired detections from unexplained alerts therefore ranks alerts by how ship-like they look (positive-unlabelled learning). Inputs are brightness, size, background texture, edge strength, compactness, distance to coast and whether the spot was seen on two other days. It was trained on 12–14 October and tested on 19 October.

5. When the AIS report is minutes old

Danish coastal AIS is seconds old. AIS heard from orbit over busy water can be several minutes old because messages collide. I rebuilt each fix from reports at least N minutes older than the image and matched with a fixed 300 m gate and with a gate that grows with report age. The reference set is moving ships the full pipeline pairs on fresh data.

Simulation and real data

The simulator came first (X-band, 10 × 10 km scenes, 45 ships, K-distributed sea, spoofed and stale AIS). It got the direction right and the size wrong: simulation had no coastline, turbines or harbours, which dominate real alerts.

Limits

  • No ground truth for which targets are dark. No precision figure is claimed on real data.
  • Sentinel-1 is C-band at 10 m pixels. An X-band Varuna-class sensor at about 1 m will see smaller boats and has its own ambiguity numbers; the geometry code reads them from the product, so the change is data, not code.
  • The ranking model learns from AIS ships, which skew large, so it ranks small boats lower.
  • Coastlines come from Natural Earth and are coarse; Swedish skerries and Wadden Sea banks leak through. Offshore substations are not in the structure list.
  • Ghost removal can hide a real boat sitting exactly one ambiguity offset from a large ship.

Two questions for the PierSight team

Does Varuna's deployable reflectarray antenna change the azimuth ambiguity ratio compared with a conventional panel? It decides how often ghosts are bright enough to become alerts.
With a 30-minute revisit, do you plan to link detections across passes? A target that moves between passes cannot be a turbine, which would replace the multi-day check I could not make work here.
